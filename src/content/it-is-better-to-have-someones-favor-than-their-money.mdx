I am 110% confident about this, and you cannot change my mind:

It is better to have someone's favor than to have their money.

That may sound like a strange thing for a businessman to say. Businesses need revenue. Employees need paychecks. Families have bills. Nobody can operate forever on smiles, handshakes, and good intentions.

I understand all of that.

But I also know that money is temporary. It comes in, and it goes out. You can earn a thousand dollars today and have an air conditioner, transmission, water heater, or emergency-room visit take it away tomorrow. Money is valuable, but it is not permanent.

The favor of a person who genuinely loves, trusts, and believes in you can last a lifetime.

## The Sale Is Not Always Worth It

One of the biggest mistakes people make in business is believing they must make every possible sale.

They see a customer with money and immediately begin figuring out how to separate that customer from as much of it as possible. They recommend products the person does not need, add services that provide no real benefit, and create urgency where none exists.

They may make the sale, but they lose something far more valuable in the process.

They lose the customer's trust.

You can talk somebody into buying something they do not need. You may even feel proud of yourself when the payment clears. But eventually, that person may realize what happened. They may feel pressured, manipulated, or taken advantage of.

Now you have their money, but you no longer have their favor.

The money will be spent. It will disappear into payroll, taxes, supplies, groceries, or another expense. Eventually, you will not even remember what you did with it.

But that customer may remember for the rest of their life how you made them feel.

That is a terrible trade.

A good businessperson is willing to say, "You do not need that."

A good salesperson is willing to recommend the less expensive option when it is the better option.

A good friend will protect your interests even when doing so costs them an opportunity to make money.

People remember that kind of honesty. They tell their friends about it. They return when they genuinely need something. Most importantly, they begin to trust you.

Trust is worth more than one transaction.

## Favor Does Not Mean Somebody Owes You

When I talk about having someone's favor, I am not talking about keeping a list of favors so you can collect on them later.

That is not favor. That is debt with a smile on its face.

True favor does not mean someone owes you anything. It does not mean you helped them move, so they must help you move. It does not mean you gave them a discount, so they must send you five referrals. It does not mean you bought lunch, so they must buy the next three.

Favor is not created through manipulation, and it should never be used as leverage.

Favor means someone loves you, trusts you, respects you, and wants good things for you. They are willing to help when life gets difficult—not because you have trapped them in an obligation, but because they genuinely care about you.

There is a tremendous difference between someone saying, "I have to help him," and someone saying, "I want to help him."

One is obligation.

The other is favor.

## Money Cannot Love You Back

I would rather be broke and surrounded by people who love me than wealthy and surrounded by people who resent me.

Money can pay a bill, but it cannot sit beside you in a hospital room.

Money can buy a house, but it cannot make that house feel like home.

Money can hire people to perform tasks, but it cannot create genuine loyalty.

Money can purchase attention, but it cannot guarantee affection.

When life falls apart—and eventually, something falls apart for all of us—the people who love us become far more valuable than the numbers in our bank accounts.

There will be seasons when you need advice. There will be times when you need someone to make a phone call, open a door, recommend your work, tell you the truth, pray for your family, or simply sit with you while you figure out what to do next.

The right person's favor can accomplish things your money never could.

They may introduce you to someone who changes your career. They may give you an opportunity nobody else would offer. They may believe in you during a season when you have stopped believing in yourself. They may help you without being asked and refuse to accept anything in return.

Not because they owe you.

Because they love you.

## Relationships Are Not Bank Accounts

Of course, this does not mean we should collect people as if they are emergency resources stored in our back pockets.

People are not assets to be managed. Relationships are not bank accounts where we deposit kindness only so we can make a withdrawal later.

If you treat people well only because you think they might be useful someday, that is still manipulation.

The point is to value the person more than the profit.

It means you do the right thing even when there is no immediate reward. You tell the truth when a lie would make you more money. You protect the relationship when exploiting it would benefit you. You serve people because you care about them—not because you are secretly calculating what they might do for you later.

Ironically, the people who stop trying to squeeze value out of every relationship often build the most valuable relationships.

They become trusted.

They become remembered.

They become the person others want to help.

## Play the Long Game

The world constantly encourages us to think about immediate returns.

How much can I make today?

What can this person do for me?

How quickly can I close the deal?

But a meaningful life—and usually a successful business—is built by playing a much longer game.

Sometimes the best business decision is to walk away from money.

Sometimes the best thing you can do is advise someone not to hire you.

Sometimes it means admitting that another company is better suited for the job.

Sometimes it means fixing a small problem without sending an invoice.

You will not always receive an immediate reward for doing those things. Some people will accept your generosity and never return. That is part of life.

But over time, character creates a reputation, and reputation creates favor.

When people know that you care more about helping them than selling them, they remember you. When they know you will tell them the truth, they listen to your advice. When they know you will not take advantage of them, they feel safe sending their friends and family to you.

One dishonest sale may put money in your pocket today.

One honest relationship may create opportunities for the rest of your life.

## Choose the Person Over the Payment

Money is useful. We should work, earn, save, give, invest, and manage it wisely. There is nothing noble about refusing to charge fairly for valuable work, and there is nothing wrong with making a profit.

The problem begins when money becomes more important than people.

Before you make the sale, send the invoice, recommend the upgrade, or pressure someone into a decision, ask yourself a simple question:

"Am I protecting this person, or am I merely trying to profit from them?"

Do work that deserves to be paid for. Charge a fair price. Make money honestly. But never sacrifice someone's trust for a transaction that will soon be forgotten.

You can take someone's money and lose their favor. Eventually, the money will be gone, and the relationship may be gone with it.

But if you treat people with honesty, kindness, loyalty, and genuine concern, you may gain something that no single payment could ever purchase.

You gain a person who believes in you.

You gain someone who wants you to succeed.

You gain someone who may stand beside you when money cannot solve the problem.

That does not mean they owe you anything. It means they love you—and love freely given will always be more valuable than money collected.

It is better to have someone's favor than to have their money.

I am 110% confident about that.

You cannot change my mind.
